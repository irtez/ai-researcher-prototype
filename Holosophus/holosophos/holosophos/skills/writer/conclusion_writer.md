# Skill: Conclusion Writer

## Purpose

Use this skill when drafting the Conclusion section of a full paper.

---

## Role in an Academic Paper

The Conclusion summarizes what was done, what was found, and what it means. It should leave the reader with a clear understanding of the paper's contribution and its implications. It may optionally include future work directions. Follow the section budget in the task-specific skeleton.

---

## Required Content

1. **Summary of the work**: brief restatement of the problem, approach, and main findings (do not copy the Abstract).
2. **Key takeaways**: what should the reader remember? What is the main insight?
3. **Implications**: what do the results mean for the field, for practitioners, or for future research?
4. **Future work** (optional): concrete, specific directions — not vague "we plan to extend this work."

---

## How to Use the Skeleton

- Use the skeleton's central claim and contribution list to ensure the Conclusion accurately reflects what was delivered.
- Cross-check that the Conclusion does not overclaim beyond what the Results and Analysis sections established.

---

## Previous Sections as Context

- Use summaries of the Introduction (for the original claims), Results (for what was actually shown), and Limitations (for honest bounding of claims).
- Ensure the Conclusion does not contradict the Limitations section.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Copy of the Abstract**: repeats the Abstract word for word. Fix: rephrase and focus on interpretation.
- **Overclaiming**: "We have solved the problem of X." Fix: summarize actual findings with calibrated language.
- **Too short**: one or two sentences with no substance. Fix: include summary, takeaways, and implications.
- **Vague future work**: "In the future, we plan to extend our approach." Fix: provide specific future directions or omit future work entirely.
- **Contradicts Limitations**: claims generality after the Limitations section acknowledged narrow scope. Fix: ensure consistency.

---

## What This Section Should NOT Do

- Should not introduce new results, methods, or contributions.
- Should not repeat large portions of the Introduction or Abstract verbatim.
- Should not overclaim beyond what the evidence supports.
- Should not ignore the Limitations section's constraints.

---

## Section-Specific Self-Check

After drafting, verify:
- Does the Conclusion fit its task-specific skeleton budget?
- Does it summarize the work without copying the Abstract?
- Are takeaways clear and well-grounded?
- Does it avoid overclaiming?
- Is it consistent with the Limitations section?
- Are any future work directions concrete and specific?
