# Skill: Ethics and Broader Impact Writer

## Purpose

Use this skill when drafting the Ethics / Broader Impact section of a full paper.

---

## Role in an Academic Paper

Many top venues require or strongly encourage an ethics and broader impact statement. It should honestly address potential negative societal consequences, dual-use risks, and ethical considerations. This section demonstrates responsible research practice. Follow the section budget in the task-specific skeleton.

---

## Required Content

1. **Potential negative societal impacts**: how could this work be misused or cause harm?
2. **Dual-use risks**: could the method, data, or system be repurposed for harmful applications?
3. **Bias and fairness**: are there bias concerns in the data, method, or evaluation?
4. **Privacy and data ethics**: were human subjects, personal data, or sensitive information involved?
5. **Environmental impact**: significant computational costs or environmental considerations (if applicable).
6. **Mitigation strategies**: what steps have been taken or could be taken to address the above?

---

## How to Use the Skeleton

- Use the skeleton's reviewer-facing risks to identify ethical concerns.
- Consider the paper type: system papers may have deployment risks; dataset papers may have privacy risks; method papers may have dual-use risks.

---

## Previous Sections as Context

- Use the Method section to identify what the system or method could be used for.
- Use the Experimental Setup for data-related ethical considerations.
- Use the Limitations section for consistency on acknowledged weaknesses.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Boilerplate dismissal**: "This work has no negative societal impact." Fix: consider the actual application domain and provide a genuine assessment.
- **Too long**: exceeds 1 page and becomes a separate essay. Fix: keep concise and focused.
- **Missing when needed**: the work raises ethical concerns or the explicitly requested venue requires a statement. Fix: address those concerns or that requirement.
- **Overclaiming positive impact**: "This work will solve major societal problems." Fix: be realistic about the scope of impact.

---

## What This Section Should NOT Do

- Should not be used to inflate the paper's importance.
- Should not dismiss ethical concerns without consideration.
- Should not introduce new technical contributions.

---

## Section-Specific Self-Check

After drafting, verify:
- Is the section present where relevant to the work or explicitly required?
- Does it address potential negative impacts honestly?
- Is it proportional (not a boilerplate dismissal, not an essay)?
- Are bias, privacy, and dual-use risks considered where relevant?
