# Skill: Related Work Writer

## Purpose

Use this skill when drafting the Related Work section of a full paper.

---

## Role in an Academic Paper

Related Work positions the paper within the existing literature. It shows the author's awareness of the field, identifies what has been done, and clarifies how this work differs. Follow the section budget in the task-specific skeleton.

---

## Required Content

1. **Topical grouping**: organize related work into 2–4 thematic groups, not a flat chronological list.
2. **Per-group summary**: for each group, summarize the key approaches, their strengths, and their limitations.
3. **Positioning**: for each group, explain how the current paper differs from or builds upon prior work.
4. **Connection to the question**: explain how the cited work informs the task; claim a literature gap only when supported.

---

## How to Use the Skeleton

- Use the skeleton's gap and contribution list to decide which areas of prior work are relevant.
- Ensure every cited work is real and verifiable. Do NOT invent citations.
- Use the evidence plan to identify which comparisons or baselines relate to prior work.

---

## Previous Sections as Context

- Use the Introduction for problem framing, gap, and contribution claims.
- Ensure the Related Work's gap narrative is consistent with the Introduction.
- Use the same terminology as the Introduction.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Flat list**: "Author A did X. Author B did Y. Author C did Z." without synthesis or positioning. Fix: group thematically and compare approaches.
- **Missing positioning**: describes prior work but never explains how this paper differs. Fix: add explicit "In contrast, our work..." or "Unlike [X], we..." statements.
- **Invented citations**: cites papers that do not exist. Fix: only cite works from provided references or verified searches. Use TODO placeholders for needed but unverified citations.
- **Too long**: exceeds the skeleton budget with unnecessary detail. Fix: retain literature needed for the actual question.
- **Defensive tone**: attacks prior work rather than positioning against it. Fix: acknowledge strengths of prior work and focus on differences.

---

## What This Section Should NOT Do

- Should not introduce the paper's own method in detail.
- Should not present any of the paper's own results.
- Should not invent or hallucinate any citations or references.
- Should not duplicate the Introduction's motivation paragraphs.

---

## Section-Specific Self-Check

After drafting, verify:
- Does the Related Work fit its task-specific skeleton budget?
- Is it organized by thematic groups, not a flat list?
- Does each group include positioning against the current paper?
- Are all cited works real and verifiable?
- Does the gap narrative reinforce the Introduction?
- Are there any invented citations?
