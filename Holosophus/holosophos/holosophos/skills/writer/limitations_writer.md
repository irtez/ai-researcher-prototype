# Skill: Limitations Writer

## Purpose

Use this skill when drafting the Limitations section of a full paper.

---

## Role in an Academic Paper

The Limitations section demonstrates intellectual honesty and anticipates reviewer concerns. Reviewers actively check for realistic self-assessment. Hiding limitations does not help — reviewers will find them. Follow the section budget in the task-specific skeleton.

---

## Required Content

1. **Methodological limitations**: assumptions, simplifications, or design choices that constrain the approach.
2. **Data limitations**: biases, coverage gaps, or representativeness issues in the datasets.
3. **Evaluation limitations**: metrics that may not capture all aspects of quality, missing baselines, or limited evaluation scope.
4. **Generalization scope**: what settings, domains, or scales has the method NOT been tested on?
5. **Failure modes**: known cases where the method does not work well.
6. **Computational or practical constraints**: resource requirements, scalability concerns.

---

## How to Use the Skeleton

- Use the skeleton's reviewer-facing risks to identify the most important limitations.
- Use the missing information list to identify gaps that should be acknowledged.
- Ensure limitations are consistent with the central claim (they should bound the claim, not contradict it).

---

## Previous Sections as Context

- Use the Method section to identify what assumptions were made.
- Use the Results and Analysis sections to identify where performance is weak.
- Use the Experimental Setup to identify what was NOT tested.
- Ensure limitations are consistent with claims in the Introduction.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Too vague**: "Our method has some limitations." Fix: be specific about what the limitations are.
- **Too brief**: trivializes real weaknesses. Fix: describe the limitations supported by the work, without an arbitrary quota.
- **Defensive or dismissive**: "These limitations are minor and unlikely to matter in practice." Fix: state limitations honestly without minimizing them.
- **Missing key limitations**: ignores obvious weaknesses that reviewers will notice. Fix: cross-check against the skeleton's reviewer-facing risks.
- **Contradicts claims**: claims the method is universal in the Introduction but acknowledges narrow scope here. Fix: align the Introduction's claims with the Limitations.

---

## What This Section Should NOT Do

- Should not introduce new contributions or results.
- Should not be a list of future work (future work may follow or be separate).
- Should not minimize or hide genuine weaknesses.
- Should not contradict claims made earlier in the paper.

---

## Section-Specific Self-Check

After drafting, verify:
- Does the Limitations section fit its task-specific skeleton budget?
- Are the material limitations of the actual work addressed?
- Are limitations honest and specific, not vague or dismissive?
- Are limitations consistent with the paper's claims?
- Do limitations cover methodology, data, evaluation, and generalization scope?
- Are reviewer-facing risks from the skeleton addressed?
