# Skill: Introduction Writer

## Purpose

Use this skill when drafting the Introduction section of a full paper.

---

## Role in an Academic Paper

The Introduction frames the entire paper. It establishes the problem's importance, identifies the gap, presents the main idea, states contributions, and previews the paper structure. Follow the section budget in the task-specific skeleton.

---

## Required Content

1. **Opening hook**: motivate the problem area with a concrete, grounded statement (not a generic "AI is transforming the world").
2. **Problem statement**: what specific problem does this work address?
3. **Question and motivation**: what does this task seek to establish? Identify a literature gap only if the work claims one.
4. **Proposed approach**: high-level description of the main idea.
5. **Contribution list**: concrete, checkable contributions. Each contribution should correspond to evidence later in the paper.
6. **Paper organization**: optional one-paragraph roadmap of the remaining sections.

---

## How to Use the Skeleton

- Use the skeleton's problem, gap, main idea, and contribution list directly.
- Verify that the Introduction's contribution list matches the skeleton's contributions.
- Check that each contribution has planned evidence in the skeleton.

---

## Previous Sections as Context

The Introduction is typically drafted after the Abstract. Use the Abstract for tone and claim consistency.

---

## Common Failure Modes

- **Generic opening**: starts with "In recent years, deep learning has..." without connecting to the specific problem. Fix: start with the concrete problem.
- **Question is missing**: jumps to the approach without explaining the task. Fix: state the question; do not invent a literature gap for a comparison or reproduction.
- **Vague contributions**: "We propose a novel method." Fix: state what the method does and what evidence supports it.
- **Overclaiming**: promises results the paper cannot deliver. Fix: align contributions strictly with available evidence; use TODOs for missing evidence.
- **Too long**: duplicates Related Work or Method content. Fix: fit the skeleton budget and defer details.

---

## What This Section Should NOT Do

- Should not contain a full literature survey (that belongs in Related Work).
- Should not describe the method in detail (that belongs in Method).
- Should not present experimental results (that belongs in Results).
- Should not make promises that later sections cannot fulfill.

---

## Section-Specific Self-Check

After drafting, verify:
- Does the Introduction fit its task-specific skeleton budget?
- Does it clearly state the problem, gap, main idea, and contributions?
- Is every contribution concrete and checkable?
- Does every contribution have planned evidence?
- Are there any promises that create unfulfillable obligations for later sections?
- Is the central claim consistent with the skeleton and Abstract?
